## What we learned

**Drum patterns are simple.** Per drum 8 patterns of 32 steps, `rec + 0xCD74 + 0x3540 d + 0x6A8 p`. The steps are four
32-byte arrays: velocity (0 = no hit), probability 0–7, sample flip (0xFF = none), microstep mask (bit k = a hit on
sixth k; several bits = a roll). Then last / first step, sync rate, direction at `+0x80`, as on note tracks. One step
has one velocity, one probability and one sample for all its microsteps. A Euclid fill is velocity + microstep bit 0
per hit; an Undo copy is 128 B (the note tracks' is 896 B).

**Stock keeps an editing copy, and it wins.** Each drum has an editor whose 32-word step store mirrors the pattern it
shows (normally the playing one). Every commit of that store, from any stock edit, a probability change or one recorded
hit, writes all 128 B back over the pattern (`0x0803EA1C`). A raw write is lost at the next edit; upstream hit this
(SC43, `drumstore.py`). Stock's own writers follow one rule: **pattern mirrored → write through the store and commit;
not mirrored → write raw.** Its Mutate (`0x0803E6F0`, called Nudge here until 2026-10-28) is the recipe to copy: raw write, the store brought in line, commit
with the editor detached (the commit then only refreshes the views). Note tracks have no such copy (PL11 writes raw).
No DSP lock and no `MARK_DIRTY`: the pattern is ARM data, saved with the record.

**Live drum hits are recorded by stock's own recorder.** Both drum views hit the drum through a live sink of the Note
view's class (`note_sink_on` / `_off`, note = drum 1–4). The router (index 4) passes it to the recorder gate, which
records only while playing with Record armed and outside the count-in. The drum recorder writes velocity, the microstep
bit (0–5 with record quantise off, 0 with it on) and probability 7, through the store when mirrored (from the Main
task). Upstream's rollers and drum arpeggiator skipped this route: they hit through the player's sink (not recorded)
and wrote the pattern raw, which is how they met the editing-copy problem.

**Shift + Drum 1–4 already reach slim.** Buttons 0–7 are Synth 1, Synth 2, MIDI 1, MIDI 2, Drum 1–4; stock's track case
`0x08033400` treats all eight alike, so `page_open`'s three stock calls work for drums, and slim's `button_press` hook
sees Shift + Drum N before stock's Shift mask (SL00D / SL01D use Drum 3 / 4 on the device). What blocks drum pages is
`chord_visible_view`, which accepts only the Note view. The Shift LEDs for Drum 1–4 are panel LEDs 36–39.

**Stock's drum views** (the track container picks one per half of the grid by a mode byte shared by all tracks):

- **Mode 1, the default.** The drum's steps are on top. Its sample pads are below, where stock's Shift + sample pad is
  a silent sample select.
- **Mode 6, the expanded view (Note pressed again).** One view serves all four drums. Pads 29–32 play drums 1–4, and
  pads 1–28 do nothing in stock; upstream put its rollers on rows 1–3.

**Each drum is one DSP voice.** A new hit ends the previous one. Upstream read the DSP voice code: **a hit softer than
the current level does not retrigger**. So note repeat with falling velocities at fast rates can be swallowed.

## What it means for CTPlay

- **Drum pages:** the page machinery carries over: `button_press` for ids 4–7 with Shift, `ctrl_pad_press` / `release`
  and `led_task` as today. They need a drum visibility test (container active, track 4–7, the drum classes) and a
  decision on the shared mode byte (a drum page opened from a note track lands in whatever mode is current).
- **Pattern tools per drum:** feasible, and smaller than the note tracks' version (128 B per Undo, ITCM). The write
  goes on the pad's release (phantom-press rule). It masks only the 128 B raw write and the store's two buffers, then
  commits unmasked, and only when the store mirrors the target pattern; the test is `[store + 0x18] == pattern + 0xA8`.
  Writing the store of a pattern it doesn't mirror would land in the wrong pattern. The pattern format offers drum-only
  options a note track lacks: rolls (microstep bits), per-hit probability, sample flips.
- **Note repeat:** use PL06's arp clock (`seq_tick` + `main_ms`, Main task, device-tested) and send each repeat through
  a drum view's live sink, note-off before each note-on. Stock should then record it like a finger press, with no
  pattern writing of ours. The press only marks "drum d held"; every hit comes from Main.
- **Saved settings and the link:** per-drum settings go in block 2 (block 1's 56 B are full). Pattern tools for four
  drums plus repeat would add about 30–36 B, so the payload goes from 81 B to about 115 B, under the link's one-byte limit
  of 127. The link's RAM budget (94 of 96 B, heap tail 40 B free) runs out first: move its last-sent image to ITCM before
  any saved field of 3 B or more (`followups.md`, PL15 final review).

## Emulation results (the three questions)

1. **The store write path works.** Raw 128 B + both store buffers + touch-all + commit gives the exact bytes; the
   step LEDs and the Velocity, Gate and Probability views repaint at once, identical to the same hits entered by stock
   presses. Our hits survive later stock edits; the control (raw write, no store sync) is wiped by one stock pad
   press. A pattern the editor does not mirror takes a raw write and stock leaves it alone. Undo by the same path
   restores bytes and LEDs.
2. **Repeats record like finger presses.** Through either drum view's live sink, from Main, playing with Record:
   with record quantise **off** every repeat lands on its own microstep (1/16, 1/32, 1/32T, even every tick); with it
   **on**, at most one hit a step. One velocity per step (the last hit's). Count-in and stopped record nothing; a
   muted drum still records; each hit takes the DSP lock once (fine from Main). The player's sink does not record.
3. **A drum button always lands in mode 1** (steps on top, sample pads below), from every start, through both the
   real button and `page_open`'s replay: the shared mode byte is set, not carried over. Note toggles 1 ↔ 6. The
   mirrored pattern equals the playing one except while a step pad of that drum is held (`[editor + 0x24]`) and after
   **Shift + Patterns** (`0x20039DE0`, likely stock's pattern-follow toggle), when the editors stay on the shown
   pattern while the chain plays on. The spec must say whether "current" means shown or playing.

## What drum-machine users expect

From manuals and forums (Reddit and Gearspace could not be read; detail and sources in the market file):

- **Note repeat ranks first.** Novation's own table lists it as the drum feature Circuit Rhythm has and Tracks
  lacks. The common design is 4 straight + 4 triplet rates (1/4–1/32T) plus latch, and the recording keeps what was
  heard. All eight rates land on the 6-microstep grid at sync 1/16. What makes it feel good: lock to the grid, a rate
  change mid-hold doesn't break the stream, swing applies, velocity moves (Tracks has no aftertouch: strike velocity,
  an optional ramp), the recording matches. Rec Quantise on squashes rates above 1/16 (emulation, above).
- **Euclid with pulses, rotate and accent velocity** is the most-used drum generator; users re-roll and hand-edit.
  Draft + Write + Undo beats Polyend (prints random fills into the steps) and Circuit's Mutate (no undo).
- **Later:** fills / trig conditions (need playback logic), ratchets with a fade across steps, combining rhythms and
  hats that avoid the kick, a Grids-style density map, flam, velocity humanize. **Not wanted:** more drum tracks,
  polymeter (stock has it), 1/64, timing humanize (a microstep is about 21 ms at 120 BPM).

## Still open (device-only, or for the spec)

- On the device: views refresh after a store write (PL11's stale-LED lesson); the DSP's no-softer-retrigger rule at
  fast rates; the unlocked router / recorder shared by finger presses (UI) and repeats (Main), as PL06's arp; a Write
  racing the recorder's commit while recording (simplest: refuse or defer a Write while Record is armed and playing).
- For the spec: "current pattern" (shown or playing); what repeat does with Rec Quantise on (say rates above 1/16
  need it off, or switch it for the hold); repeat velocity (strike, fixed, ramp); which panel setting forces live
  drum velocity to 96; swing and sync rates other than 1/16 for repeat.
